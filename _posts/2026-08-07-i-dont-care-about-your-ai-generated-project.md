i dont care about your ai generated project and i'm feeling particularly scornful about it after seeing two projects today that i really wanted to share before seeing that there was little to no human involvement in their development.

lots of people are posting lots of cool things that they have prompted a large language model to create. lots of my time gets wasted trying to figure out if there is a human with intention and skill behind a project; or if the thing is the product of channeling machine spirits via a [federal-money-pit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JnX-D4kkPOQ)-subsidized monthly credit. 

set aside all your moral, [legal](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/03/whos-legally-to-blame-for-anthropic-and-openais-autonomous-ai-hacks-its-complicated/), [intellectual-property](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQw4w9WgXcQ), [environmental](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eZchOopvunQ), [risk-tolerance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=obmlZH3X9gs), [economic](https://www.pcgamesn.com/wp-content/sites/pcgamesn/2025/12/december-2025-ram-price-rise.jpg), [geopolitical](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qItugh-fFgg), and [long-term-tech-debt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I-u8fo5esYI) related qualms with ai for a moment. lets say that ai Rules, actually, and nobody's ever had their tap water collared by a surveillance datacenter that will never have the budget to upgrade to a closed-loop system. lets just say that for a moment and let's fully accept that AI as it exists has no externalities to speak of. 

there are two kinds of software projects i care about:

### you made a tool. 


that's awesome. maybe it saves you time, maybe it makes a difficult task easier. that's a totally valid use-case for LLM generation—no need to waste more time on a means to an end than you've gotta!

but like—why do i care about your tool? what do i actually want to read about your tool? 

if it's directly relevant to me then maybe i'll take an interest to the use-case and download it. but so many cool tools exist that i wouldn't ever need to use, so it's safe to assume this one's not really gonna change my life either. 

despite that i would love to hear about the challenges you faced while making the tool. your design considerations and your UX iteration. tell me how your tool scales or about wacky ways to use the thing. tell me about the rough edge your tool had that you smoothed over after some big breakthrough you synthesized while watching Breaking Bad. let me learn from not just your artifact but from your methodology.

except, you didn't think about any of those things. you prompted a robot and gave it maybe an example invocation, a sketchy drawing, or described a few of its command line flags. but more likely you iterated with the robot to figure out how the tool would work at all. 

i don't want to read your chat logs. i can pay claude three dollhairs and recreate your logs and your whole tool in the time it takes me to get lunch. hearing this process teaches me nothing. seeing your >1k LoC artifacts only tells me that you voluntarily opted to send money to a frontier inference provider. samuel alterman, dario amodei, along mask, and tongy stark need that cash. and you needed to purchase some thought.

i'll be at the south indian lunch buffet while my rip of your tool is being created. an improvement in every promptable way—hashtag yolo mode bros! i'll have a second agent iterating on the friction in common user stories, a third agent iterating on security vulnerabilities, and a fourth agent just refreshing your tool's github to make sure i never lose feature parity. i won't know how to use either of our tools until i read the readme or feed it back to a bot to summarize. i learned nothing. i filled a gas town ralph wiggum jira board with alphabet soup. my tool will beat your tool's ass though, because i threw an extra few loops at it. $3? $300. but mine installs autonomously, just copy and paste this easy prompt directly into your agent. $3? no, $6, you had to rerun the install because it stochastically decided to purge your local npm packages instead.

the only way to break the curse is to do crochet and play video games that have a no-AI disclaimer attached. or, like, seriously consider logging off.
